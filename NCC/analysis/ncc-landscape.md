# NCC 2026 Landscape

Status date: April 22, 2026

## Fixed conference facts

- Conference: The 10th National Conference on Communications (NCC 2026)
- Host: Makerere University CEDAT with UCC
- Venue: Makerere University, Kampala
- Dates: June 24-26, 2026
- Theme: Accelerating Market-Driven Innovation for Uganda's Development
- Submission deadline: May 1, 2026
- Acceptance notification: May 22, 2026
- Public submission categories:
  - Full research papers: up to 8 pages
  - Extended abstracts: ongoing work
  - Industry case studies
  - Policy and position papers

## Public tracks

From the official poster and CFP page, NCC 2026 is organized around eight tracks:

1. Digital Infrastructure, Connectivity and Future Networks
2. Digital Financial Services, FinTech and Economic Inclusion
3. Digital Content, Media, Broadcasting and Creative Innovation
4. Artificial Intelligence, Data Science and Trustworthy Technology
5. Smart Societies: E-Government, Urban Innovation and Public Services
6. ICT for Social Impact: Health, Education and Human Development
7. Agriculture, Environmental Resilience and Digital Empowerment
8. Policy, Regulation, Cybersecurity and Innovation Ecosystems

## What NCC publicly wants in 2026

The official launch language is more specific than the CFP language. The strongest repeated ideas are:

- Market-driven innovation, not research for its own sake
- Practical solutions that can be tested against industry realities
- Clear bridges between academia, industry, government, and investment
- Scale, jobs, services, and inclusive growth
- Solutions tied to national priorities and real-world constraints

The most important public framing from the UCC launch page is that NCC should connect:

- knowledge to opportunity
- innovation to investment
- talent to impact

That means a paper that is only technically clever is weaker than a paper that is technically sound and deployment-aware.

## What NCC publicly rewarded in 2025

The 2025 official recap matters because it is the closest visible precedent:

- Over 120 technical papers were submitted
- Best papers were approved for publication on an international journal platform
- Moderated panels, industry keynotes, and innovation showcases were part of the judging environment
- Post-conference focus areas were:
  - practical scaling of innovations
  - stronger university-industry partnerships
  - wider regional participation
  - measurement of socio-economic impact

The 2025 winning paper set also shows what "good" looked like:

- a machine learning deployment problem for telecom infrastructure
- an IoT-cloud transport payments system
- a smart electricity metering system
- a connectivity and solar-powered research-access paper

The common thread is applied, buildable, locally legible innovation.

## Grant and publication mechanics

Two public signals matter:

- The NCC 2025 recap says the best papers were approved for publication in an international journal platform.
- UCC's 2025 Terms of Reference for evaluating NCC says accepted papers are published in conference proceedings and a project support grant is awarded to the best peer-reviewed paper(s).

Important caution:

- The public NCC 2026 CFP does not disclose the current grant amount or the exact 2026 evaluation rubric.
- UCC's April 2026 research-support call is separate from NCC, but it is still a very useful proxy for how UCC currently defines proposal quality, strategic alignment, implementation planning, and public-value output.

## Formatting and process caveats

- The public manuscript template page on `ncc.co.ug` is still branded around `NCC-25` and `IJBEST`.
- Use that page as a formatting baseline only, not as proof that the 2026 publication outlet is unchanged.
- The masterclass page on the public NCC site also still references `NCC Conference 2025`, which suggests some author-guidance content may lag the current edition.

## What this means for our paper

- We should write for Track 1 first, with Track 4 as the fallback.
- We should frame the work as a low-cost infrastructure efficiency play, not just an ML classifier.
- We should make the operator and regulator use case explicit.
- We should show measurable service benefit, not only model accuracy.
- We should include a credible path from experiment to field utility.
