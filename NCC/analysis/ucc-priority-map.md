# UCC Priority Map

Status date: April 22, 2026

This map synthesizes what UCC has been emphasizing publicly in late 2025 through April 2026.

## 1. Inclusive digital economy, not connectivity alone

UCC's April 2026 research call says its 2025/26-2029/30 strategic-plan vision is `An Inclusive Digital Economy` with a goal of accessible, affordable, and secure digital services by 2030.

Why it matters:

- Papers should not stop at technical performance.
- UCC is looking for work that increases real participation in digital life.

Implication for us:

- We should present denoising as a way to recover usable service in places where weak or noisy connectivity blocks inclusion.

## 2. Access gap plus usage gap

The December 2025 sector-report article is very clear:

- Uganda has growing subscriptions and data use
- but affordability, digital skills, and lack of relevant local content still limit meaningful use
- coverage gaps remain, especially in hard-to-reach areas

Why it matters:

- UCC does not view signal availability alone as success.
- It cares about whether people can actually use services.

Implication for us:

- Position the paper around `usable coverage` and `effective service reach`, not abstract RF coverage.

## 3. Market-driven innovation and investment logic

The NCC 2026 launch repeatedly frames the conference as a bridge from:

- knowledge to opportunity
- innovation to investment
- talent to impact

Why it matters:

- UCC wants research that can survive contact with industry realities.

Implication for us:

- We should explain who would adopt the system, what it saves, and how it could fit operator or regulator workflows.

## 4. Stronger academia-industry-government collaboration

This theme appears across:

- NCC 2026 launch language
- the 2025 NCC recap
- the UCC research-support call

Why it matters:

- UCC favors work that connects universities to deployment environments and real sector problems.

Implication for us:

- We should explicitly frame the work as useful to UCC, UCUSAF, MTN, Airtel, and future field pilots.

## 5. Spectrum integrity, compliance, and harmful interference

UCC's 2024 crackdown on illegal and non-compliant broadcasters highlights:

- signal interference with licensed operators
- impact on quality of service
- effect on aviation and security users
- enforcement against illegal equipment and non-compliant transmission behavior

Why it matters:

- Signal integrity is not a niche issue for UCC; it is central to regulation and public safety.

Implication for us:

- Our project should directly speak to interference diagnosis, weak-emitter interpretation, and support for cleaner spectrum operations.

## 6. Next-generation infrastructure and underserved-area access

The April 2026 satellite-regulation seminar shows UCC is actively thinking about:

- remote and underserved connectivity
- new access models such as LEO satellite systems
- spectrum management
- licensing, competition, consumer protection, affordability, and sovereignty

Why it matters:

- UCC is not locked into one technology stack; it is thinking broadly about infrastructure reach.

Implication for us:

- Our paper fits if we present it as a complementary software-side tool for getting more value from existing and future coverage investments.

## 7. Affordability and telecom taxation

The tax-policy study highlights:

- taxes on data, devices, and network infrastructure
- affordability and usage effects
- rural users being especially affected
- the need for a balanced framework that improves growth and investment

Why it matters:

- Low-cost, software-first improvement narratives fit this environment better than capex-heavy narratives.

Implication for us:

- We should emphasize that denoising-driven effective coverage extension may improve service without immediately requiring new towers or new spectrum allocations.

## 8. Sustainability and green transition

The February 2026 energy-footprint study shows UCC is now visibly focused on:

- telecom energy consumption
- carbon implications of network growth
- practical, phased recommendations toward cleaner operations

Why it matters:

- UCC is rewarding ideas that improve service while respecting efficiency and sustainability concerns.

Implication for us:

- We can add a secondary angle that recovering more usable coverage from existing assets is potentially more resource-efficient than brute-force expansion alone.

## 9. Proposal quality and implementation discipline

The April 2026 research-support call and the 2022-2026 framework place heavy weight on:

- strong problem definition
- realistic timelines and resource needs
- implementation plans
- cross-cutting issues
- outputs that matter to policy and sector development
- proposals written clearly for reviewers who may not be narrow specialists

Why it matters:

- Even a technically strong idea can lose if it reads like an unfinished lab note.

Implication for us:

- Our future draft must be structured, specific, and written for mixed reviewers.

## Best-fit narrative for our project against UCC priorities

The strongest UCC-aligned version of our story is:

- Uganda still has noisy and weak service zones in underserved areas
- operator and regulator decisions in those zones are evidence-constrained
- a denoising-first signal pipeline can recover more usable information from weak/noisy GSM environments
- that improves troubleshooting, interference interpretation, and effective service reach
- the approach is software-forward, lower-cost, and relevant to inclusive digital access
